# The first driven captures: where the band was, what scale the model chose, and what a stopped car measures

Seven capture sessions on 13 September 2026, a phone at the side window of a car at 63–90 km/h
along a dual carriageway, 41 segments measured through the deployed pipeline (`terrain,vegetation`,
`--offset 2`, the grid's defaults). Twelve segments produced no cell at all, another twelve fewer
than ten, and the readings that did come out ran 0.02–0.15 m where the operator expected more.
Everything below was measured on a local bench that re-runs `scripts/assess-grass.mjs` on the
`scene.glb` and `result.npz` the RunPod handler leaves beside each segment's frames, with no GPU
and no change to the depth stage. The bench, its scripts and its logs live outside the
repository in `D:\greenv-runs\`; the packets it produced are reproducible from the same objects.

### The band was on the road side of the track in every usable segment — 2026-09-13

**In 37 of 37 segments with a usable reconstruction, 100% of the back-projected vegetation mask
lay on the negative side of the camera track — the side a positive `offsetM` never reaches.**
`D:\greenv-runs\scale.mjs all`, column "negative side", on every mirrored run; the four
exceptions are the collapsed tracks below, which have no side. On the reference segment
`01a09b0f/1` (run `20260913-140757-87b58d`, 102 frames) the lateral profile of the mask
(`lateral.mjs --every 4`, 889,436 back-projected points) starts at −5 m from the camera, runs to
−9 m at heights of 0.04–0.17 m above the plane, climbs an embankment from −10 to −19 m, and puts
its remaining fifth at −34 to −41 m and 4–12 m up: the tree line. Nothing at all on the positive
side. The shipped band — polyline 2 m to the positive side, 5 m wide, both sides folded — spans
−3 to +7 m and contains only asphalt, which the mask excludes. Retained points per frame: 0 in
all 102 frames, in production and in the local reproduction (`measure.mjs 20260913-140757
--offset 2`: 0 cells of 0, byte-identical outcome).

Flipping the sign alone (`--offset -2`) on all 41 runs: the twelve zero-cell segments became
85–566 measured cells each; over the 37 usable runs the median segment went from 2 measured
cells to 276 (`sweep-left.log`). Distance is as much the problem as side: the near verge sits
5–9 m from the camera behind a shoulder and a guardrail, and no fixed offset knows that.

### DA3's per-clip scale ran from 0.78x to 1.86x against the GPS path of the same frames — 2026-09-13

**The camera track on the fitted plane was shorter or longer than the vehicle's own odometer
by a factor between 0.78 and 1.86 across 37 segments of one drive, median 1.24.** The frame
extractor stamps every sampled frame with `distanceMeters`, its GPS path distance from the
segment's start, so the span between first and last sampled frame is the length the track ought
to have. On `01a09b0f/1` that span is 29.8 m against a 23.3 m reconstructed track: the model's
metres are 22% short, and so is every height measured in them. The per-segment table is in
`scale.log` and the comparison in this session's transcript; the camera's height above the plane,
which a fixed mount keeps constant, scattered from 0.93 to 2.25 m across the same segments and
did not become constant after correction, so it is not the anchor — the track length is.

Correction is one scalar per run, applied to the alignment matrix, the camera centres and the
plane's offset together, and recorded in the packet under `scale` with the model's own numbers
beside the factor. Factors outside 0.5–2 are refused with the blocker `scale-anchor-unusable`:
on this day those were the segments whose track curled (`01a09b11/2`, arc 56.8 m for 14.6 m end
to end) or collapsed, and stretching a wrong shape only makes a bigger wrong shape.

### A camera that did not move measures a door handle at 1.57 m — 2026-09-13

**Four segments reconstructed a camera track of 0.5, 0.7, 1.8 and 6.6 m against 30–170 m
driven, and the pipeline reported them as 1.57, 1.26, 1.42 and 3.93 m of vegetation.** The
contact sheets (`sheet.mjs`) show why: in `01a09b0f/0` and `01a09b10/0` the first 95 frames are
the car's door and dashboard while the phone is being mounted; in `01a09b15/0` the car is stopped
at a painted chevron and the only vegetation is a tree; `01a09b19/0` likewise. With the whole
cloud a metre across, whatever the mask touched — a door handle, a mirror, a tree — filled the
ten cells of a 0.5 m grid and became the segment's tallest reading. The gate `minTrackM` refuses
a track shorter than the caller's floor with `camera-track-too-short` and no cells; the
deployment sets 3 m.

### What the automatic band, the anchor and the gate give together

`scripts/grass-anchor.mjs` (pure, tested) and three request fields in `runGrassPipeline`:
`offsetSide: "auto"` places the road edge at the 10th percentile of the mask's lateral distance
less 0.5 m, on the side that holds at least 70% of it, and widens the band to the 90th percentile
within 5–10 m; `trackLengthM` anchors the scale; `minTrackM` gates the track. Defaults leave
every one of them off, so a walked fixture measures exactly as recorded. On the reference
segment the three together give 659 measured cells of 833, band at −7.2 m and 10 m wide, scale
×1.28, `extent95M` p50 0.16 m and p95 0.33 m, and the near verge's cells (0–3 m from the edge)
read 0.12–0.22 m with 19–24 frames each while the embankment's own ground climbs from 0.42 to
1.10 m and its extents stay at 0.09–0.19 m — the per-cell datum following the slope as designed.
`check-grass-quality.mjs` passes on the packet. The 41-run sweep with all three on is in the
section below.

### A wet road hides the floor, and a coarser fit finds it — 2026-09-13

**Segment `01a09b11/13` (run `20260913-150211-232fa1`) measured nothing because no ground plane
was found: the best hypothesis held 0.80% of the cloud against the 1% floor.** Rain, and a road
that reflects the sky; the depth model reads the reflection as depth scattered below the
surface, so along gravity the cloud's 1st percentile sits 0.90 m under its median where the
healthy run `0f/1` has 0.47 m (`floor.mjs`). The true ground is a thin layer no 3.5 cm plane
can gather 1% of. Halving the support floor recovers a plane at 5.4° with 0.8% inliers; twice
the inlier distance (0.07 m) gives 1.6% at 5.7°, RMSE 0.041 m, 1.4% of the cloud below it, and
the same track length as every other tolerance tried (29.1 m). The camera stands 2.2 m above
each of them, which is a height a car camera can have and a wall or a reflection cannot.

`groundFallback: true` makes that one coarser attempt when the strict fit fails, keeps it only
if the camera stands 0.3–6 m above the result, and records `ground.relaxedFit` and the blocker
`ground-fit-relaxed`. With it and the vehicle options the segment measures 601 cells of 815:
`extent95M` p50 0.28 m, p95 0.57 m, band 5.9 m out on the negative side, scale ×1.37. Off by
default; the deployment turns it on.

### The frames float, a crown floats, and the corridor is five metres — 2026-09-14

**A mown verge the photographs put at 10–15 cm read 16–28 cm because the frames of a driven
capture do not agree about where the ground is.** On `0f/1`, `11/13` and `7e/1` a cell's
heights spread 1–8 cm *within* one frame (per-frame `h95 − h50`) and 24–52 cm *between* frames
(`h95SpreadM`); `7e/1`, where the float was 5–11 cm, was the one verge that read 4–8 cm. The
pooled datum takes the 2nd percentile of every voting frame together — the lowest floating
frame — while the canopy percentile is the median frame's, so half the float became grass.
`datum: "per-frame"` measures each frame's percentiles against that frame's own 2nd percentile
and takes the median across frames: `0f/1` p50 0.16 → 0.09 m, `11/13` 0.28 → 0.11, `7e/1` 0.08
→ 0.04. `geometry/grass-height-grid.test.ts` pins it on five frames of one lawn, three of them
floated 0.30 m: pooled reads over 0.30 m of grass, per frame reads the lawn.

**A branch at two metres passes under a 3 m point ceiling and inside a 2 m extent.** `7e/3`
read a p95 of 1.58 m from the tree beside the road. What separates a crown from a hedge or tall
grass is not height but the air under it: a frame sees the ground, then nothing for half a
metre or more, then foliage, where a plant is continuous from the ground up. `canopyGapM: 0.5`
— the widest gap between consecutive voxel heights with a tenth of the voxels above it, median
across frames — sets 27 cells of `7e/3` aside as `floating-above-ground` and its p95 falls to
0.99 m with the 10 m band, 0.30 m with the corridor below. No cell of `0f/1` is touched.

**The band followed the vegetation out to 10 m and climbed the slope behind the corridor.**
`19/5` measured its mown strip and the brush above it together, p95 1.13 m. Five metres from
the road edge is what a crew cuts; `maxDistanceFromRoadM: 5.5` from the auto-placed edge, which
sits half a metre before the vegetation starts, holds the band to that: `19/5` 1,804 → 1,489
cells, p95 0.82 m.

The three together on the 43 runs (`sweep-final.log`, `compare.py --tags canopy,final`): 40
measured, 17,699 cells, 26 set aside as canopy; p50 2–18 cm on 37 of the 40, 27 cm on `19/2`
and 41 cm on the stopped car of `15/0`; p95 6–92 cm; dashboard levels 7 × 1, 11 × 2, 22 × 3
where the previous round had 34 × 3. The p95 that decides the level is the top twentieth of the
cells: a hedge or a touceira at the corridor's edge is enough to hold a mown strip at level 3,
and whether the level should follow p50, p95 or the share of cells above the threshold is the
policy question `GRASS-QUALITY.md` leaves to Motiva.

**Scale, checked against the guardrail.** In `0f/1` the cloud's height profile in the lateral
strip [−3.6, −1.8] m from the camera ends sharply at 0.51–0.52 m above the plane: the top of the
W-beam, which the standard puts at 0.70–0.75 m. That asks for ×1.35–1.45; the GPS anchor gave
×1.28. The two agree, and neither is what inflated the verge. `19/5`'s ×1.66 puts its vegetation
10 m from a camera whose photograph shows a narrow shoulder, and stays an open question.

### The rail's lower edge, and the wet road on the wrong side of the line — 2026-09-14

**On `11/7` the twelve tallest cells, 40–57 cm on a strip that reads 3–9 cm, are the last half
metre against the guardrail.** Their own pixels painted back on frame 74 (`cellpix.mjs`) sit
along the rail's lower edge: at 128×128 logits a class pixel is 4.5 by 8 photograph pixels, and
the `terrain` beside a `fence` carries the rail with it. `excludeNearClasses:
"fence,wall,pole,building"` with a one-pixel radius drops 15,922 of 278,345 mask pixels on that
run and takes the segment's p95 from 31 to 29 cm; a three-pixel radius would take it to 24 cm
but starts on vegetation beside any wall, so one it is. What stays above 30 cm there is the
metre of grass a mower does not reach under the rail — 15–45 cm, and real.

**In rain the road is `vegetation`.** The class map of frame 9 (`mask.mjs`) gives the wet
asphalt to `vegetation` for a fifth of the frame: the reflection of the trees. The band folded
both sides of the edge together, so the road side entered as cells of 0 cm. `bandSide`, set by
the auto-placed edge to the vegetation's side, keeps only that side; 17,699 cells become
17,060 across the 43 runs, p50 moves by at most 1 cm on 40 of them, and four segments whose p95
sat just past 30 cm from those rows step down to level 2 (`0f/1` 39 → 29, `10/1` 41 → 15,
`11/2` 32 → 30, `11/7` 31 → 29). Levels 6 × 1, 14 × 2, 20 × 3.

### The corridor ends where the ground starts to climb — 2026-09-14

**`19/5` measured its mown strip and the embankment above it together, and the embankment
won: p95 0.82 m on a strip the photograph shows cut short.** A bank is where each cell's own
ground climbs away from the road plane, so `slopeRiseM` walks outward along every along-road
column of cells and calls the first cell whose ground rises by more than that from the cell
before it, *and* whose next cell rises by more than that again, the slope's foot; that cell and
everything beyond it are `slope`, kept with their numbers, aggregated nowhere. Two steps rather
than one because a kerb or a raised median is one step and then level ground — with one step
`11/7`'s mown strip lost 147 of 365 cells to a "slope" that was its own kerb; with two it loses
25. At 0.1 m per half-metre cell (a 20% grade) on `19/5`: 460 cells set aside, p95 0.82 → 0.17
m, p50 0.08 → 0.04; on the flat `7e/1` nothing changes; on `0f/1`'s gentle bank five cells.
0.075 catches gentler banks and starts on kerbs (121 cells on `11/7`), so 0.1 it is.

On the 43 runs (`sweep-slope.log`): 17,060 cells to 15,100, 1,971 set aside as slope, 40
measured, p50 2–20 cm on 39 of them (46 cm on the stopped car of `15/0`).

**And the stretch stood for its 90th percentile for a few hours, not its 95th.** The top
twentieth of a corridor's cells was its last half metre against the rail or the touceira at its
far edge, and by p95 the same 43 runs sat at 6 × 1, 14 × 2, 20 × 3; by p90 at 9 × 1, 21 × 2,
10 × 3. The change was in GreenV's API projection
(`JacksonMeasurementProjectionAdapter.STRETCH_PERCENTILE`), not in this pipeline, whose per-cell
`extent95M` is unchanged; the API's field keeps the name `extent95P95M` because the installed
capture app reads it. **It went back to the 95th the same day**, once the rail stopped being
measured — see the last section of this page.

### A structure the model never learned, and the corridor's end — 2026-09-14

**`11/10` read a p90 of 0.59 m on a median strip between two guardrails in the rain, and
`7e/2` 0.55 m on a mown lawn behind a concrete barrier; both photographs show grass of 5–15 cm.
The tall cells are the rail and the barrier themselves, and the model calls them grass.** Not
the depth: in `11/10`'s tallest cell (35.75 m along, 0.75 m out) twenty frames put the 95th
percentile at 0.96–1.04 m above the plane, and in `7e/2`'s (15.25 m, 0.25 m) thirteen frames
at 0.63–0.83 m, agreed to a few centimetres — a real object 0.7–0.8 m tall, seen the same from
every frame. `cellpix.mjs` paints the tallest cells' own pixels on the rail's face and on the
barrier's, and `classpix.mjs` (the class map drawn EXIF-rotated, as the depth grid is) paints
both `terrain`: frame 100 of `11/10` has the near rail green with `fence` on 0.4% of the frame
at the far rail, frame 23 of `7e/2` has the barrier green and no `wall` anywhere. Cityscapes
carries a *guard rail* label that is not among the nineteen classes this model was trained to
predict, so a rail is whatever its neighbours are. The same objects are `fence`, `wall` or
`building` in other frames — the frames of `11/10` carry a median 170 structure pixels each
(12–604), those of `7e/2` 312 (0–5,143) — which is what a pixel margin around those classes
(`excludeNearPx`) could not use: it only reaches the frames that saw one.

**So each frame's structure pixels are back-projected into the same cells as its grass, whole
(no erosion, a pole is one pixel wide), and a cell that `structureFrames` frames landed
`minVoxelsPerFrame` structure voxels in is `structure`, kept with its numbers, aggregated
nowhere, and left out of the slope search (a rail's own P2 is its lower edge, half a metre up).**
Three frames, the bar a cell must clear to be measured. On `11/10` the cross-tabulation is
what set it: 78 cells over 40 cm, 55 of them seen as a structure by three or more frames, 14 by
one or two, 9 by none; 22 cells under 20 cm were seen by one or two frames, so a bar of one
would refuse 22 short cells to reach 14 tall ones.

**And nothing past the camera track's ends is measured.** `11/10`'s last along-road column
(40.25 m) was 65–75 cm at every distance from 1.25 to 5.25 m while the column before it was
mown: `stationOf` folds a point past the polyline's end onto that end with the overshoot turned
into distance, and the rail one metre out reappears at every distance up to the band's width. A
walked polyline spans its stretch and the fold is right for it; a driven edge is the camera
track and stops at the last pose. `pastEnds: "drop"` leaves those points out.

**On the two runs** (`packets/<run>/structure`, `sweep-structure-two.log`): `11/10` 355
measured cells to 317, 75 `structure`, p90 0.59 → 0.20 m, p50 0.09 → 0.07. `7e/2` 281 to 234,
41 `structure`, p90 **0.55 → 0.55**. Drawn cell by cell along the road, `7e/2`'s barrier line
is refused in its first half, where `wall` appears in three frames or more, and measured in its
second — a W-beam guardrail there, not the barrier — where no frame calls it anything but
terrain: 46 cells over 40 cm remain, 31 of them with no structure frame at all, every one at
0.25–1.25 m from the edge in one continuous line, the lawn beyond 1.75 m at 3–6 cm.

**What was tried for that second half, and did not separate it** (all on the same packets):
the model's own confidence (`confpix.mjs`) — on frame 23 the barrier's pixels win at a median
0.90 with `wall` the runner-up for 87% of them, on frame 60 the rail's win at 0.97 with
`vegetation` and `sidewalk` the runners-up, as the lawn's do; a stacking metric (voxels per
2 cm ground column) — 0.02–0.04 m in every group, structure, tall and short, on four runs,
because the depth noise spreads a face over several columns; a ground step — across column
24.25 m the cells' own ground is 13–15 cm at 0.25, 0.75, 1.25, 1.75 and 2.25 m while the
extent is 4, 39, 57, 3, 5 cm, so the lawn behind the barrier is at road level in the
reconstruction and only the extent jumps; and the model at 1024 × 1024 input instead of 512 —
frame 23's barrier becomes `wall` on top and `sidewalk` on its face (4.1% of the frame `wall`
where 512 gave none) and leaves the mask, frame 60's rail and `11/10`'s stay `terrain`, at
811–973 ms a frame against 138. A 0.6 m stand of vegetation and a 0.6 m rail are the same
shape to the depth model; only appearance tells them apart, and this model's classes stop
short of a guardrail.

### A second model that knows a fence — 2026-09-14

**`7e/2` reads a p90 of 0.05 m with a SegFormer trained on ADE20K asked what is not grass;
it read 0.55 m with the Cityscapes model alone.** Cityscapes never trained its models on a
guardrail; ADE20K's 150 classes include `fence`, `railing`, `wall` and `bannister`, and MSeg's
relabelling of ADE20K found its guardrails inside `fence` (`mseg-api`,
`relabeled_data/ade20k_fence_guardrail`). On the frames of this page (`adepix.mjs`, EXIF-rotated
like the depth grid): `Xenova/segformer-b2-finetuned-ade-512-512` paints the rail of `7e/2`
frame 60 `fence` (2.8% of the frame) and the barrier of frame 23 `wall` (8.3%), the rail of
`11/10` frame 100 `fence` (3.3%), all of which the Cityscapes B0 painted `terrain`; the ADE20K
B0 does not (frame 60: `grass` over the rail, no fence). B4 finds a little more (frame 60: 4.0%)
and runs no slower than B2 on this CPU — 869 against 886 ms a frame, median over five frames of
`7e/2`, `adetime.mjs`; the decoder is the cost — so B4 (246 MB) is the one pinned, at
`0f92f0b465567a1a1f004ec33110992b6158edbc`. At 1024 × 1024 input it takes 12.8 s a frame for
0.9% of `fence` where 512 gave 0.1% on `11/10` frame 85: not an option.

**Wired as a second opinion, not a replacement.** The Cityscapes model still decides what is
grass, so every number calibrated on it stands; the second model (`structureModel`) is asked
only where its structure classes (`structureClasses`, in its own names) lie, and those pixels
join the structure map: out of the grass mask with the `excludeNearPx` margin, and into the
cells `structureFrames` counts. A pixel is a structure to it when the probability it gives
those classes, summed, reaches `structureFloor` (0.5 unless asked), because ADE20K spreads a
rail over four classes and no one of them need win. The packet records the model, its
revision, its classes, its floor, its pixels and its time on every frame
(`frame.semantic.structureModel`).

**On the four runs** (`packets/<run>/ade`, `sweep-ade-four.log`, the winning class rather than
the floor): `7e/2` p90 0.55 → **0.05 m**, p50 0.05 → 0.03, max 0.60 → 0.21, measured cells 234
→ 95 with 125 `structure` — the whole barrier-and-rail line at 0.25–1.25 m from the edge, the
lawn beyond it still measured at 3–6 cm. `11/10` p90 0.20 → 0.16 m, 88 `structure`; its last
ten metres keep 15 cells over 40 cm because in the fog of frames 85 and 96 the rail is `grass`
to the ADE20K B4 too, and only frame 100 sees it. With both models voting, a bar of two frames
instead of three would take those to 7 for 21 cells of short grass, and one frame to 6 for 40;
the bar stays at three. The second model's median is 1,132 structure pixels a frame on `7e/2`
and 31 on `11/10`, at 1.0 s a frame.

**The floor, and two other models tried the same way.** Summing the second model's
probability over its structure classes and asking for 0.4 of it rather than the winning class
took `11/10` from 0.16 to **0.14 m** for ten cells of 303 (`packets/…/ade04`,
`sweep-ade-floor.log`) and changed nothing on `7e/2`; 0.4 is the deployment's floor. Two more
models were wired the same way and measured on the same four runs (`sweep-clip-four.log`,
`sweep-vistas-four.log`). CLIPSeg (`Xenova/clipseg-rd64-refined`, fp16, prompted with
"guardrail", "guard rail" and "concrete barrier") sees the rail in fog and rain where the
SegFormers do not — 0.78–0.84 on frames 85 and 96 of `11/10` — and is the cheapest on two
ONNX threads, 0.75 s a frame against the ADE20K B4's 3.9 s (1.28 s on four, `threads.mjs`);
but its 352 × 352 answer spills a metre onto the grass around the rail, and through the
pipeline it left 50 measured cells of 317 on `11/10` and 59 of 234 on `7e/2` for p90s of 0.11
and 0.06 m. A MaskFormer trained on Mapillary Vistas (`onnx-community/maskformer-resnet50-vistas`,
the one public label set that names `Guard Rail` and `Barrier`; its semantic map computed
here from its hundred queries) paints the barrier of frame 23 `Barrier` end to end and the
lawn behind it untouched, and on `7e/2` it is the better answer — the band lands on the lawn
and 303 cells read p50 0.07, p90 0.09 m — but its quarter-resolution masks fatten below a
W-beam: on `11/10` 51 cells of 317 survived for a p90 of 0.11 m and on the flat `7e/7` 116 of
225, with the retained points per frame down from 10,248 to 3,345. At 2.0 s a frame on four
threads. Both stay registered (`clipseg`, `vistas-r50`) for the record; `ade20k-b4` is
deployed.

**On the 43 runs** (`sweep-ade.log`, floor 0.4): 10,069 measured cells where the class veto alone
left 10,924 and the slope round 15,100; 4,634 cells `structure`, 1,870 `slope`; levels 18 × 1,

### The two stretches still tallest, and a model that votes without masking — 2026-09-14

**With the ADE20K model deployed the tallest stretches are `11/1` (p90 0.30 m in the cloud,
0.24 on the bench, 34 cells) and `11/8` (0.24 m, 150 cells), and in both the tall cells are
again a structure the second model does not see.** `11/1` is a concrete barrier, a strip of
grass a metre wide and a wall; the ADE20K B4 paints the barrier and the wall `earth` and `land`
(frame 17: 4.8% and 3.1% of the frame, `fence` 0.3%, `wall` 0.1%), the Mapillary MaskFormer
paints them `Barrier` 5.3%, `Building` 3.8%, `Wall` 1.6%. `11/8` is a strip between two

9.5%) where the MaskFormer sees `Guard Rail` 7.2%. The tallest cells' own pixels (`cellpix.mjs`)
lie on the strip against the far rail in `11/8` and against the wall in `11/1`.

**The MaskFormer's cost was its mask, not its classes, and the mask can be kept out of the
grass.** Its masks are a quarter of the frame's resolution and fade a metre onto the grass
beside a rail; taken out of the grass mask with the margin in every frame, that fade starved the
median strip of `11/10` of its points (125 measured cells of 293, floor 0.7). Neither a hard cut
on each query's mask at 0.5 (`vistasm07`: 69 cells — in the fade the competing `Terrain` mass

### Not established here

No reading on this page has been compared with a tape; the day produced no reference
measurement. A guardrail both models call grass in a frame — the wet rail in fog at the end of
`11/10` — is measured as vegetation in that frame, and only the frames that see it can refuse
the cell; fifteen such cells over 40 cm remain there with the ADE20K model. A strip narrower
than two metres between two rails is measured by whichever model is deployed from the pixels
its masks leave, and no model tried here resolves its edges at a quarter of the frame. The Mapillary Mask2Former Swin-L, the
best masks measured on these frames (`Guard Rail` 8.0% of frame 60, `Barrier` 10.0% of frame
23), takes 8–23 s a frame on CPU and transformers.js cannot load it; on the GPU that already
runs the depth stage it would be cheap, and that has not been tried. The band's far edge is a percentile of a mask that includes tree lines, so the 10 m
cap is doing real work and has not been graded. The scale anchor trusts the phone's GPS odometer
over 30–40 m with 2–5 fixes per segment; the extractor's `distanceMeters` is its own
interpolation of those fixes, and its error has not been measured. And the measurement still
covers the first 30–40 m of each 170–250 m segment (`groups[].published` in the manifest), which
is a coverage decision outside this pipeline.
